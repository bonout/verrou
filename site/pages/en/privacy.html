Privacy

Privacy policy.

Last updated: 2026-10-25. If this page differs from the English version on GitHub, the English version prevails: PRIVACY.md.

Verrou is a client for a Vaultwarden (Bitwarden-compatible) server that you choose and run, or that someone you trust runs for you. Verrou's developers operate no server, collect no data and have no access to your vault.

What Verrou collects

Nothing. Verrou has no account of its own, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no tracking. The app's privacy manifests declare no collected data.

Where your data goes

  • Your Vaultwarden server: your vault, encrypted on your device before it is sent (Bitwarden's end-to-end encryption), and site names for website icons. Your server's operator is responsible for what it stores.
  • Have I Been Pwned ("Pwned Passwords", api.pwnedpasswords.com): when the breach check runs, only the first 5 characters of a password's SHA-1 hash are sent (k-anonymity), never the password or the full hash. The new password check can be turned off in Settings.
  • Your iCloud account, only if you pair an iPhone to approve the Mac's requests: the requests and answers go through your own iCloud private database (CloudKit), sealed end to end between your two devices, then are erased; Apple's push notification service tells the iPhone a request is waiting, without its content.
  • GitHub, for the Mac app downloaded from the website: once a day and when you choose "Check for Updates…" (automatic checks can be turned off in Settings), Verrou downloads the description of the latest release and, if it is newer, its disk image. GitHub sees your IP address and the version of Verrou and of macOS (in the request's User-Agent); nothing else is sent.
  • The site of an item, only when you tap "Change Password" or "Create a Passkey" for it: Verrou asks that site whether it publishes the standard addresses for those pages (/.well-known/change-password, /.well-known/passkey-endpoints), without cookies.

On your device

The vault is stored encrypted on your device; on iPhone it is excluded from iCloud and computer backups. Face ID and Touch ID are handled by the system: Verrou never sees your biometric data. The camera is used only to scan a one-time code's QR code, and no image is saved.

On iPhone, the one-time codes you choose to show in widgets ("Show in widgets and on Apple Watch" on an item's page) are kept in this iPhone's keychain, outside the vault, until you turn this off or sign out: their title, site and seed, never synced and never sent anywhere. If you have an Apple Watch with Verrou, the iPhone hands the same list to it directly (Apple's WatchConnectivity, between your paired devices), and the Watch keeps it in its own keychain: nothing leaves your Apple devices. When you pick a vault item in a shortcut, the Shortcuts app keeps its title and identifier; Verrou adds nothing of your vault to Spotlight.

When Verrou crashes or hangs, macOS and iOS hand it a report (MetricKit) about once a day: call stacks and versions, nothing from your vault. Verrou keeps the 20 most recent on the device and never sends them; Settings → Diagnostics show how many there are, let you share them (you choose with whom) or delete them.
